# Positioning

Copy fails when positioning is vague. Fix the positioning first; every other launch skill reads its output.

## Inputs (ask in one message for whatever is missing)

- What the product does, in the user's words
- Who has used it so far (even 3 people) and what they said
- What those people did before (spreadsheet, competitor, hiring someone, nothing)
- Price or intended price

## Steps

1. **Current alternative** — name what the buyer uses today. "Nothing" is valid only if the problem is new; otherwise it is usually a spreadsheet, a manual process, or a bigger tool they half-use.
2. **Switching trigger** — the moment the alternative hurts (end of month, a new hire, a failed deadline). Positioning is aimed at that moment.
3. **Differentiator** — at most 2 capabilities the alternative lacks. Each must be checkable in a demo, not an adjective.
4. **Audience** — the narrowest group that has the trigger often and can pay. Job title + context ("solo bookkeepers with 10–40 clients"), not "small businesses".
5. **Statement**: `For [audience] who [trigger], [product] is a [category] that [differentiator]. Unlike [alternative], it [proof].`

## Tests (run all three, report pass/fail)

- **Swap test**: replace the product name with a competitor's. If the statement is still true, the differentiator is wrong.
- **Stranger test**: could someone in the audience repeat it after one read? Under 30 words?
- **Evidence test**: is each claim backed by a user quote, a number, or a demo step? Mark any that are not.

## Output

1. The statement.
2. Test results with the fix for any failure.
3. A 6-word headline candidate and a 15-word subheadline derived from it, for the landing-page-copy skill.
4. One sentence: the riskiest assumption in this positioning and the cheapest way to check it this week.
